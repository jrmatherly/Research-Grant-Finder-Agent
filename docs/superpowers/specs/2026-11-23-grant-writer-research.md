## NIH policy on AI in applications

- **NOT-OD-25-132**, "Supporting Fairness and Originality in NIH Research Applications", released 2025-07-17, in force from the 2025-09-25 receipt date. Checked. <https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-25-132.html>
  - NIH "will not consider applications that are either substantially developed by AI, or contain sections substantially developed by AI, to be original ideas of applicants."
  - "AI tools may be appropriate to assist in application preparation for limited aspects or in specific circumstances."
  - Detection after an award: possible referral to the Office of Research Integrity, disallowed costs, withheld awards, suspension, termination.
  - Six new, renewal, resubmission or revision applications per investigator "for all council rounds in a calendar year". The notice exempts T and R13. The Grants Policy Statement (revised March 2026, section 2.3.7.12) and the FAQ also exempt R25. Counting is by Council-date year. Agent-read.
- **"Substantially developed" is not defined** in the notice, in the Grants Policy Statement section 2.3.7.13, or in the reminder below. Agent-read.
- **Reminder of 2026-05-14** (NIH with the Office of Research Integrity). Checked. <https://grants.nih.gov/news-events/nih-extramural-nexus-news/2026/05/helpful-reminders-to-ensure-integrity-of-nih-supported-research-when-using-artificial-intelligence>
  - Editing or polishing text is acceptable under institutional, journal or funder policy.
  - Fully generating an application, where the output "may have incorrect, misleading, or copied information", may be treated as a concern.
  - An AI-generated reference that does not exist, presented as real, could be data fabrication. Copying substantial text without disclosure could be plagiarism.
  - Applicants should "clearly describe" their use of AI tools "including a section on described methods for reproducibility". No form field is named.
- **No field for an AI-use statement.** The notice, the policy statement, the reminder, and the FORMS-I instructions for research, career and fellowship applications name none. A search of the three instruction files for "artificial intelligence", "generative", "AI" and "Super Intelligence" found nothing. Agent-read. Whether one institute or one announcement asks for a statement is unverified. Reading Section IV of the target announcement settles it.
- **A live FAQ disagrees.** `https://search.grants.nih.gov/faq/api/faq/678` still says NIH "only prohibits" AI in peer review and that applicants use it "at your own risk". The answer predates the notice. The notice and the policy statement govern. Agent-read.
- **Reviewers.** NOT-OD-23-149 (2023-06-23) bars reviewers from using generative AI on applications. Agent-read.
- **Terminology.** NIH's pages carry a banner: under Executive Order 14434, agencies use "Super Intelligence (SI)" in place of "AI". Search for both. Agent-read.
- **Other funders.** NSF (notice 2023-12-14, page updated 2026-05-21) encourages proposers to say in the project description how they used AI. The NEH (policy file dated 2024-10) permits AI and requires AI text to be marked with footnotes. DOE, NASA, USDA and DoD are unverified. Agent-read.
- **Privacy.** NIH's confidentiality statements are aimed at reviewers. No NIH statement was found that tells applicants not to put their own unpublished ideas into a third-party AI service. Agent-read.
- **Not settled.** Any 2026 Guide notice that amends NOT-OD-25-132. The agent's search found none, which may be its query. The check is the weekly index at <https://grants.nih.gov/funding/nih-guide-for-grants-and-contracts>.

## NIH application rules

Sources: the page-limits table (updated 2026-09-30), the format page, and the FORMS-I instruction files released December 2025. All agent-read unless marked.

- Page limits: <https://grants.nih.gov/grants-process/write-application/how-to-apply-application-guide/page-limits>
- Format: <https://grants.nih.gov/grants-process/write-application/how-to-apply-application-guide/format-attachments>
- Instructions: `https://grants.nih.gov/grants/how-to-apply-application-guide/forms-i/{research,career,fellowship}-forms-i.pdf`

### R01, R21, R03

| Section | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Project Summary/Abstract | 30 lines of text |
| Project Narrative | Three sentences |
| Introduction (resubmission or revision) | 1 page |
| Specific Aims | 1 page |
| Research Strategy | 12 pages for R01, 6 for R21 and R03 |

- "R21 page limit may be different when combined with other activity codes. For example, R21/R33."
- A limit absent from the table, from Section IV and from any notice means no limit. The announcement overrides the table, and a Guide notice overrides both.
- Research Strategy starts each part with the heading Significance, Innovation, or Approach. Preliminary studies sit inside it and count. "Early stage investigators should include preliminary data."
- Abstract: stand-alone, states long-term objectives, aims, health relatedness, design and methods. "Avoid both descriptions of past accomplishments and the use of the first person." No proprietary or confidential information.
- Narrative: "relevance of this research to public health in, at most, three sentences".
- Format: 11 points or larger, at most 15 characters per inch, at most six lines per inch, margins at least 0.5 inch, letter paper, no headers or footers, flattened PDF, file name of 50 characters or fewer.
- "Figures should not be included in the Specific Aims attachment." The abstract and the narrative "should only include text (no figures)".
- "Hyperlinks and URLs are only allowed when specifically noted in funding opportunities and/or form field instructions."
- A resubmission marks changes with no brackets, highlighting, bold or margin lines.

## Where the announcement text comes from

All checked on 2026-11-23 by running the request.

- `NIH_GUIDE_API_URL` in `config.py` (an AWS execute-api host) returns 24 to 26 keys per record and no body, with `fields=all`, with `fields=doctext`, and with no `fields`. None of the 369 saved NIH Guide records has a body.
- `https://search.grants.nih.gov/guide/api/data`, with the same parameters, returns the same record with `doctext`. For PAR-27-090 it is 79,030 characters of plain text with "Section I." twice, "Section IV." three times, "Section V." twice, and `ggid` 360675. The field is undocumented.
- Grants.gov `fetchOpportunity` for id 360675 returns `synopsisAttachmentFolders[].synopsisAttachments[]` with one attachment: id 355218, `PAR-27-090-Full-Announcement.html`, `text/html;charset=UTF-8`, 155,608 bytes.

- NOT-OD-25-143 (2025-08-14): from fiscal 2026 NIH stopped posting announcements in the Guide, and Grants.gov is the official source. Agent-read.
- NIH RePORTER returns `abstract_text`, `phr_text` and `terms`, and no aims or application text. One request per second, large jobs at night or on weekends. Agent-read.

### Anthropic Claude

Checked against the Claude API reference that ships with Claude Code, for the endpoint, the headers, the version, the streaming event names, schema output, and the model identifiers and prices.

- `POST https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages`. Headers `x-api-key`, `anthropic-version: 2026-10-21`, `content-type: application/json`.
- Body `{"model", "max_tokens", "system", "messages": [{"role": "user", "content"}]}`.
- Reply `content` is a list of typed blocks. Read the blocks whose `type` is `text`. `stop_reason` is `end_turn`, `max_tokens`, `refusal`, and others. `usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens`.
- On version `2026-10-21`, a request that sends `temperature`, `top_p` or `top_k` returns 400. So does a prefilled assistant turn.
- A safety classifier can decline a request: HTTP 200 with `stop_reason: "refusal"` and a `stop_details` category such as `bio` or `cyber`. A server-side `fallbacks` parameter exists (beta header `server-side-fallback-2026-07-01`, value `"default"`). Its raw-HTTP form was not read. <https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/refusals-and-fallback>
- Streaming: `"stream": true`. Events `message_start`, `content_block_start`, `content_block_delta` (text in `delta.text` when `delta.type` is `text_delta`), `content_block_stop`, `message_delta`, `message_stop`, `ping`, `error`.
- Schema output: `"output_config": {"format": {"type": "json_schema", "schema": {...}}}`. Objects need `additionalProperties: false`. No recursion, `oneOf`, or length limits.
- Models: `claude-opus-5-5` ($4 and $20 per million input and output tokens), `claude-sonnet-5-5` ($2 and $10), `claude-haiku-5-5` ($0.10 and $0.50), `claude-fable-5-2` ($8 and $40). All have a 1M context and 128K output.
- `GET /v1/models` lists models. The page's example uses `Authorization: Bearer`. Whether `x-api-key` also works there is unverified.
- Errors: `{"type": "error", "error": {"type", "message"}, "request_id"}`. 401, 403, 429 `rate_limit_error`, 500, 529 `overloaded_error`. `retry-after` in seconds.
- Data: not used for training by default. Deleted within 30 days. Zero retention by arrangement, and not on Fable models.
- The `anthropic` package (2.12.0) brings 15 transitive packages, two of them compiled.

### OpenAI

- The documentation recommends the Responses API for new work, and says Chat Completions "remains supported". Self-hosted servers implement Chat Completions.
- Chat Completions: `POST https://api.openai.com/v1/chat/completions`, `Authorization: Bearer`, body `{"model", "messages": [...], "max_completion_tokens"}` (`max_tokens` is deprecated). Text at `choices[0].message.content`. `finish_reason` is `length` when cut off.
- Responses: `POST /v1/responses`, body `{"model", "instructions", "input"}`. Text in `output[]` items of type `message`, in `content[]` items of type `output_text`. It stores state for 30 days unless the request sends `"store": false`.
- Models: `gpt-6.2-astra`, `gpt-6.2-sol`, `gpt-6.1-terra`, `gpt-6-luna`. `GET /v1/models` lists them.
- Errors: fields `error.code`, `error.type`, `error.param`. `Retry-After`. A 429 for quota or credit is not fixed by a retry.
- Data: not used for training unless opted in. Abuse logs for up to 30 days.
- The `openai` package (3.57.0) brings 13 transitive packages.

### Google Gemini

- The documentation recommends the Interactions API for new work and calls `generateContent` legacy but supported. The pages moved from ai.google.dev to aistudio.google.com/docs.
- `POST https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/interactions`. Headers `x-goog-api-key`, `Api-Revision: 2026-05-20`. Body `{"model", "system_instruction", "input"}`.
- Text is in `steps[]` items of type `model_output`, in `content[]` items of type `text`. `status` is `completed`, `incomplete` or `failed`. A raw reply sample was not read.
- Interactions are stored by default (55 days on a paid plan, 1 day on the free tier). Send `"store": false`.
- Models: `gemini-4-carbon` ($2 and $10, doubling on 2026-12-01), `gemini-4-hydrogen`, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`. `GET /v1beta/models` lists them.
- Errors: `{"error": {"code", "message"}}`. 429 `rate_limit_exceeded` or `quota_exceeded`, 503. No retry header is documented.
- Data: a paid plan does not use prompts to improve products. The free tier does, human reviewers may read them, and the terms say "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services." Guaranteed zero retention needs Vertex AI.
- The `google-genai` package (3.6.1) brings 25 transitive packages.

## How the design was chosen

Three candidates designed the whole feature from one grounding document, on three seats (one Fable, two Opus). A fourth model (Opus) judged them against six criteria and read the code to check their claims.

| Criterion | Candidate 1 | Candidate 2 | Candidate 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reads as original design | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| Existing behaviour is safe | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| Rules are data | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| Provenance by construction | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| Deterministic checks are pure | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Size and fit | 3 | 3 | 3 |

- All three chose an append-only revision log as the storage, with everything else derived from it.
- Candidate 1 is the base. SQLite refuses a model revision with no logged call and any change to a revision, and one `Workspace` class is the only writer surface.
- Candidate 2 let a draft become section text with no applicant action. That breaks the owner's first decision, and it was not taken.
- Candidate 2's Profile tab sat behind the dashboard's empty-results `st.stop()`, so a new user could not reach it. The plan uses a view selector that runs before that guard.
- Candidate 3 kept the bare-word "AI" rule and the generic terms hard-coded, so a wet-lab profile would still get the AI bonus. The plan derives both from the profile.
- The judge found that the fallback reason "Weak computational signal; included for monitoring only" kept its wording under a custom profile in all three. The plan relabels it.
